This section provides clarifications for commonly misunderstood or frequently questioned rules. These rulings supplement the *Traveller Core Rulebook* and establish how certain mechanics are handled within this campaign.

When a rule is unclear, use the interpretations presented here.

---

## Characteristics & Modifiers

Traveller Characteristics and their associated modifiers are separate values.

When determining a Characteristic Modifier, use the Characteristic's normal modifier rather than treating the Characteristic score itself as the modifier.

For example, a Traveller with **EDU 7** has an **EDU modifier of +0**.

The modifier is what is applied to checks and other mechanics that call for the Characteristic Modifier.

### Characteristic Modifiers

| Characteristic | Score | Modifier |
|---|---:|---:|
| — | 0-2 | -2 |
| — | 3-5 | -1 |
| — | 6-8 | +0 |
| — | 9-11 | +1 |
| — | 12-14 | +2 |
| — | 15+ | +3 |

> [!warning] Score vs. Modifier
> A Characteristic score of **7 does not provide +7** to a roll. It provides the modifier associated with a score of 7, which is **+0**.

## Skills & Training Levels

Skills are tracked by their **Skill Level**, beginning at 0.

A Traveller's level of training determines what they can reasonably accomplish with a skill.

The progression is:

**Untrained → 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5+**

### Untrained

The Traveller has no training in the skill.

When attempting a check with an **Untrained** skill, the Traveller suffers **DM-3** to the check.

This penalty represents the difficulty of attempting a task without any formal training.

A Traveller with levels in **Jack of All Trades** may reduce this penalty. Each level of Jack of All Trades reduces the Untrained penalty by **1**, to a minimum penalty of **DM-0**.

For example:

- **Untrained, Jack of All Trades 0:** DM-3
- **Untrained, Jack of All Trades 1:** DM-2
- **Untrained, Jack of All Trades 2:** DM-1
- **Untrained, Jack of All Trades 3+:** DM-0

> [!info] Jack of All Trades
> Jack of All Trades does not grant actual training in a skill. Instead, it represents a Traveller's broad experience and ability to attempt unfamiliar tasks without suffering the full Untrained penalty.

### Skill Level 0

The Traveller possesses basic familiarity with the skill.

A Skill Level of 0 does **not** provide a bonus to a roll, but it allows the Traveller to use the skill normally.

### Skill Level 1+

Each additional level represents progressively greater training and expertise.

A Traveller with Skill Level 2 is more capable than one with Skill Level 1, while a Traveller with Skill Level 3 is more capable still.

> [!info] Important
> Skill levels and Characteristic modifiers are separate. A Traveller may have **Skill 0** and still benefit from a positive Characteristic Modifier.

# Encumbrance

Under ordinary circumstances, the Referee should not require Travellers to track every item they carry.

A reasonable amount of equipment can simply be assumed to be carried without issue, such as:

- Armour
- A primary weapon
- A pistol
- A few grenades
- Items contained in a backpack

Encumbrance becomes important when a Traveller begins carrying a significant amount of equipment.

### Carrying Capacity

A Traveller can carry equipment totaling a number of kilograms equal to their combined:

**STR + END**

without penalty.

A Traveller may carry up to **twice** this amount, but suffers **DM-2 on all physical actions** while doing so.

The Referee may also call for Fatigue checks when a Traveller is performing heavy labour or carrying a substantial load for an extended period.

### Armour

The mass of worn armour is distributed around the body when properly worn.

For the purposes of encumbrance, reduce the listed mass of armour being worn by **75%**.

For example, armour with a listed mass of 20kg counts as only **5kg** toward a Traveller's encumbrance while properly worn.

# Augments from Character Creation Benefits

Some character creation benefits grant access to cybernetic implants or other augmentations.

When a benefit states that a Traveller may select augmentations up to a specified value, the Traveller may select **multiple augmentations** so long as their combined value does not exceed the listed limit.

For example, a benefit granting **Cr75,000 worth of augmentations** may be spent on:

- One augmentation worth Cr75,000
- Two or more augmentations whose combined value is Cr75,000 or less
- Any combination of eligible augmentations within the Cr75,000 limit

The benefit is not restricted to a single augmentation unless the benefit specifically says otherwise.

# Ship Shares

Ship Shares normally represent ownership or equity in a starship.

For *Prometheus*, the Referee may allow Ship Shares to be applied toward the purchase of a **vehicle** instead.

Each Ship Share applied to a vehicle reduces the purchase price by **10% of the vehicle's total cost**.

### Example

A Traveller has **3 Ship Shares** and wishes to purchase a vehicle costing **Cr100,000**.

Each Share is worth 10% of the vehicle's price:

**Cr100,000 × 10% = Cr10,000**

Three Ship Shares therefore provide:

**Cr30,000** toward the vehicle.

The remaining **Cr70,000** must be paid normally.

### Paying Off Vehicle Shares

When Ship Shares are used to purchase a vehicle, the Traveller assumes the equivalent of a mortgage on the remaining value.

The remaining amount is paid off as though the vehicle were a starship, divided across a **10-year mortgage**.

The Referee may use the normal Traveller mortgage rules to determine the exact payment schedule.

# Standard of Living

*Prometheus* uses the **Standard of Living** rules from the *Traveller Core Rulebook*.

Standard of Living represents the ordinary expenses required to maintain a Traveller's lifestyle, including food, accommodation, utilities, clothing, and other routine necessities.

| Standard of Living | Cost/Month | Suitable For |
|---|---:|---:|
| Very Poor | Cr400 | SOC 2 |
| Poor | Cr800 | SOC 4 |
| Low | Cr1,000 | SOC 5 |
| Average | Cr1,200 | SOC 6 |
| Good | Cr1,500 | SOC 7 |
| High | Cr2,000 | SOC 8 |
| Very High | Cr2,500 | SOC 10 |
| Rich | Cr5,000 | SOC 12 |
| Very Rich | Cr12,000 | SOC 14 |
| Ludicrously Rich | Cr20,000+ | SOC 15 |

A Traveller's Standard of Living covers ordinary food and living expenses while residing in civilized areas.

Specialized requirements, such as the Sanguophage's need for blood or a Waster's need for Psychite, are **not** automatically covered by Standard of Living unless specifically stated otherwise.
