Establishing a permanent colony is a major undertaking. These rules provide a simple framework for managing a colony without requiring the players or GM to track individual residents, resources, or daily activities.

The colony is treated as a single entity with a small number of attributes representing its overall capabilities.

---

# Establishing a Colony

When the Travellers establish a new colony, determine its initial **Population Capacity** by adding together the Social Standing (SOC) scores of all founding Player Characters.

**Initial Population Capacity = Combined SOC of the Founding Travellers**

The colony's actual starting Population is determined by the circumstances of its founding.

### Example

Four Travellers establish a colony with the following SOC scores:

**SOC 7 + SOC 6 + SOC 8 + SOC 5 = 26**

The colony therefore begins with a **Population Capacity of 26**.

---

# Population

**Population** represents the number of permanent residents living within the colony.

Every resident is assumed to have suitable housing as long as the colony remains within its Population Capacity.

Individual housing requirements do not need to be tracked.

Population may increase through immigration, recruitment, refugees, natural growth, or other circumstances determined by the GM.

A colony cannot normally exceed its current Population Capacity.

---

# Population Capacity

**Population Capacity** represents the maximum population the colony can sustainably support with its current residential infrastructure.

Population Capacity begins with the combined SOC of the founding Travellers.

**Housing Upgrades** increase the colony's Population Capacity.

Housing is therefore not tracked as a separate colony resource. When a Housing Upgrade is completed, the colony's maximum sustainable population increases by the amount specified by that upgrade.

If the colony reaches its Population Capacity, additional residents cannot normally join the colony until its Population Capacity is increased.

---

# Colony Attributes

Every colony has five primary attributes:

| Attribute      | Description                                             |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Food**       | The colony's ability to provide food and clean water.   |
| **Power**      | The colony's ability to generate and distribute energy. |
| **Healthcare** | The colony's ability to provide medical care.           |
| **Economy**    | The colony's ability to generate and maintain wealth.   |
| **Security**   | The colony's ability to protect itself from threats.    |

These attributes represent the overall capabilities of the colony rather than individual resources.

Buildings and upgrades can increase or decrease these attributes.

---

# Food

**Food** represents the colony's ability to provide sufficient food and clean water for its population.

A colony must have sufficient Food to support its Population.

If Food production is insufficient, the colony may suffer shortages and their associated consequences.

The GM determines the severity of these consequences based on the circumstances and duration of the shortage.

---

# Power

**Power** represents the colony's ability to generate and distribute energy.

Facilities may require Power to operate.

If the colony does not have sufficient Power to support its infrastructure, some facilities may need to be shut down or rationed.

The GM determines which facilities are affected.

---

# Healthcare

**Healthcare** represents the colony's ability to provide medical treatment to its population.

A colony with insufficient Healthcare may struggle to treat serious injuries, illnesses, and other medical problems.

A colony with sufficient Healthcare can provide routine medical treatment and support the recovery of its residents.

---

# Economy

**Economy** represents the colony's overall ability to generate wealth and maintain its standard of living.

A strong Economy allows the colony to purchase supplies, maintain infrastructure, and fund expansion.

A weak Economy may leave the colony dependent on outside assistance or vulnerable to shortages.

---

# Security

**Security** represents the colony's ability to protect its population and infrastructure from threats.

A colony with insufficient Security is more vulnerable to raids, hostile factions, criminal activity, wildlife, and other dangers.

A strong Security makes the colony better able to resist and respond to threats.

---

# Colony Tech Level

Unlike Settlements, a colony does not have a single Tech Level.

Individual buildings, facilities, and upgrades have their own Tech Level requirements.

A colony may therefore contain infrastructure from several different technological eras.

The presence of an advanced facility does not automatically increase the Tech Level of the entire colony.

---

# Buildings and Upgrades

Buildings and upgrades are the primary method of developing a colony.

Each building or upgrade provides its own:

- Cost
- Tech Level
- Construction Time
- Requirements
- Effects

Buildings and upgrades may increase Colony Attributes or Population Capacity.

The complete list of available buildings and upgrades can be found in **Colony Buildings & Upgrades**.

---

# Construction

To construct a building or upgrade, the colony must meet all of its listed requirements and pay its listed Cost.

Construction takes the listed amount of Construction Time.

Once construction is completed, the building or upgrade provides its listed effects.

Player Characters may participate in construction when appropriate utilizing their downtime to reduce the time it takes to complete construction of the project.

---

# Colony Turns

Colony management is handled on a longer timescale than normal character actions.

A **Colony Turn** normally represents one real time month.

Routine activities during a Colony Turn are abstracted. The colony's population is assumed to perform ordinary work necessary to maintain the settlement.

Player Characters become involved when something requires their direct attention.

Examples include major shortages, attacks, construction projects, technological discoveries, or other significant events.

---

# Colony Problems

A colony may encounter problems when its capabilities are insufficient to support its population.

Common problems include:

- Population reaching its Population Capacity
- Insufficient Food
- Insufficient Power
- Inadequate Healthcare
- Economic difficulties
- Insufficient Security

These conditions do not necessarily result in immediate penalties.

Instead, they create circumstances that may develop into complications, events, or adventures if left unresolved.

The GM determines the consequences based on the severity of the problem.

---

# Colony Growth

A colony grows by increasing its Population Capacity and improving its capabilities.

Housing Upgrades increase Population Capacity.

Other buildings and upgrades improve the colony's Food, Power, Healthcare, Economy, and Security.

As the colony develops, increasingly advanced infrastructure may become available.

---

> [!tip] Colony Management in Brief
> **1. Establish the colony's initial Population Capacity using the combined SOC of the founding Travellers.**
>
> **2. Determine the colony's starting Population.**
>
> **3. Track the colony's Food, Power, Healthcare, Economy, and Security.**
>
> **4. Construct buildings and upgrades to improve the colony.**
>
> **5. Use Housing Upgrades to increase Population Capacity.**
>
> **6. Resolve major problems and events as they arise.**
>
> **7. Continue expanding the colony as its population and capabilities grow.**
>
> Individual housing, daily resource consumption, and routine labour do not need to be tracked.
