## An Empire in Name

The Empire that rose from the ashes of Sophiamunda is not what it once was. What calls itself the Empire today is a **collection of planet-states** ruled by noble houses. They still fly the old banners, still use the old titles, and still insist that they are one great and undivided realm.

They are not. Each world governs itself, keeps its own armies and pursues its own interests. The Imperial Throne is respected, invoked and sworn to, but distance and relativistic travel mean its commands arrive slowly, if they arrive at all. Like the late empires of old Earth, the Shattered Empire lives on its name, its rituals and its memory of greatness, long after the reality behind them has fractured.

The pretense still matters. The banner of the Empire is what gives each lord the right to rule, so no house will let it go.

## The Houses and the Stellarchs

Beneath the Emperor, real power belongs to the **Stellarchs**, who govern whole regions, and to the planetary nobles and lesser aristocrats who serve them. In practice they behave like **competing feudal lords**. They stand together under the Imperial banner for legitimacy, and fight one another for everything else: territory, trade, titles and favour.

The common saying among Imperial courtiers sums it up: _"We are all on the same side. I simply deserve a larger share of it."_

Open war between houses is rare, but it happens. Far more common are:

- assassinations and duels of honour
- trade embargoes and proxy wars
- marriage alliances
- quietly hiring off-world mercenaries to do what a noble cannot be seen doing

Travellers who find themselves useful to one house will almost certainly become enemies of another.

A Stellarch's word is law in their region. Laws, bans and bounties can differ from one Stellarch's domain to the next, but all of them claim the authority of the Throne.

## Imperial Law

### Who Made These Laws?

**The Stellarch.** The laws enforced in the Imperial settlements on Prometheus were decreed by the Stellarch whose region includes Prometheus, in the name of the Throne. Imperial officials won't hear them questioned. To them, an edict from the Stellarch carries the full weight of the Emperor's own word, however harsh it is.

### One Legitimate Government

The Empire recognises **no government but itself**. As far as Imperial law is concerned, every world in the galaxy is rightfully Imperial territory, whether or not the Empire controls it, and every crime committed anywhere is a crime against the Empire.

The Empire will not cross the stars to hunt a Traveller down for something done on a distant, lawless world. But once that Traveller **enters Imperial reach**, the crime is treated as if it happened on the Emperor's doorstep. "It happened somewhere else" is not a defence an Imperial court recognises.

### Weapons

**Only soldiers and members of the nobility may bear arms, and only while on duty.** Commoners, travellers, mercenaries and off-worlders may not carry weapons of any kind within an Imperial settlement.

Weapons are surrendered at the gate or confiscated on discovery. Being caught armed is grounds for arrest at best.

### Proscribed Persons

Some people are criminals simply for **existing** within Imperial territory:

- **Pirates and raiders**, including anyone known to have sailed with them.
- **Awakened Androids.** The Empire calls them _deviants_. The standing order across nearly every Imperial domain is **destruction on sight**. Unawakened Androids are tolerated only as property.
- **Carriers of restricted xenogerms**, most notably **Sanguophages**. Possessing, carrying or spreading a restricted xenogerm is a capital crime.

### Aiding and Abetting

Harbouring, travelling with, employing or protecting a proscribed person is a crime in its own right. An entire crew can be condemned for the company it keeps.

### Punishment

Imperial justice is **swift, public and cruel**. Sentences commonly include:

- flogging
- flaying
- execution by firing squad

Lesser offenders may be fined, branded or pressed into service. Greater offenders rarely leave the settlement alive.

## Xenohumans and Synthetics

The Empire is a human empire first. Most **xenohumans are second-class citizens**: tolerated, taxed and useful, but denied the rights and protections of baseline humans. A few favoured xenotypes are accepted into Imperial society, and some even serve in its military or its noble households. These exceptions prove the rule rather than break it.

**Synthetics fare worst of all.** Under Imperial law, Androids are property. An Android that awakens and claims to be a person is regarded as a malfunction at best and an abomination at worst.

---

## Friends in High Places

Imperial justice is harsh, but it is not blind. It answers to the nobility, and the nobility answers to its own interests.

A Traveller with a **noble patron**, an Imperial officer who owes them a favour, or friends of high enough standing can often have charges dropped, sentences commuted or inconvenient questions forgotten. The same law that would flay a commoner can be waived for a valued servant of a powerful house.

Not every Imperial is a zealot, either. Some are pragmatists, some are rebels at heart, and some simply have friends they would rather not see hanged. Travellers fortunate enough to meet such people should treasure them. They are the exception, not the rule.

## The Empire on Prometheus

**Prometheus is not an Imperial world.** Most of the planet is lawless frontier, and outside Imperial walls no Imperial law applies.

That changes at the gates of an **Imperial settlement**. The Empire holds a number of settlements, estates and enclaves on Prometheus, where noble expeditions, diplomatic missions and scientific outposts operate under full Imperial law. Within them, every rule on this page applies.

The larger the settlement, the more dangerous it becomes for anyone on the wrong side of that law. A small Imperial outpost may lack the soldiers to enforce every edict. A proper Imperial settlement does not. Pirates, Awakened Androids, Sanguophages and those who travel with them should expect to be **shot, flayed, flogged, or all three**.

## Referee Notes

- **Law Level:** treat Imperial settlements as **Law Level 9** for anyone who is not a soldier or noble, meaning all weapons are prohibited. For the CSC Black Market rules, that gives **DM-1** to Streetwise checks, and failed checks have harsher law enforcement responses.
- **Checkpoints:** entering an Imperial settlement usually means a document and weapons check. A proscribed Traveller who wants to slip through can use **Deception**, **Streetwise** or **Admin** checks against forged papers or a bribe. If they are caught, roll on the CSC **Law Enforcement Response** table using the settlement's Law Level.
- **Social Standing:** within Imperial territory, **SOC** matters more than anywhere else. Travellers with SOC 10+ (minor nobility) may be permitted to bear arms. Xenohumans may suffer DM-1 or worse on social checks with Imperial officials, depending on their xenotype and the local Stellarch.
- **Patronage:** a noble Contact or Ally is the single most valuable asset a Traveller can have in Imperial territory.
